📝 Application Guide — Step by Step

Start 8–12 months before your target intake (September is the main one; January and May are smaller).

📅
1. Pick your intake & programSeptember (Fall) is the main intake; January (Winter) and May (Summer) are offered by many unis and colleges. Shortlist 3–5 programs at designated learning institutions (DLIs).
🗂️
2. Prepare documentsAcademic transcripts, passport, English test result, statement of purpose, letters of recommendation, CV and proof of funds.
🗣️
3. Meet the English requirementTypically IELTS 6.5 overall (no band below 6.0) for degrees, 6.0 for many diplomas. TOEFL, PTE and Duolingo are widely accepted — check your program's page.
💵
4. Show proof of fundsYou must prove you can pay tuition plus living costs — the IRCC minimum is roughly CAD 20,635/year, usually shown via a Guaranteed Investment Certificate (GIC) from a participating bank.
📜
5. Get your acceptance & apply for the study permitAccept your offer, get the letter of acceptance, then apply online for a study permit on the IRCC site. Fee: CAD 150 (biometrics extra for most applicants).
🛡️
6. Arrange health coverage & housingProvinces have different health plans (e.g. UHIP in Ontario, MSP wait in BC) — confirm before you fly. Book housing early; September demand is intense.
✈️
7. Book travel after approvalFly only after your study permit is approved. Carry your letter of introduction, acceptance letter and fund proof — the border officer issues your actual permit on arrival.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to study in Canada in 2026?
International bachelor's tuition in Canada typically runs CAD 20,000–50,000 per year (about $14,000–$36,000 USD), master's CAD 17,000–40,000, and college diplomas CAD 13,000–20,000. You must also show funds for living costs (the IRCC minimum is roughly CAD 20,635 per year via a GIC) plus the study permit application fee of CAD 150. Always confirm current figures on canada.ca.
What is a study permit and how do I get one?
A study permit is the document that lets you study at a designated learning institution (DLI) in Canada. You need a letter of acceptance from a DLI, proof of funds for tuition and living costs, and to apply online through the IRCC website before you travel. The fee is CAD 150 and most applicants also need biometrics.
Can international students work while studying in Canada?
Yes — full-time students with a valid study permit may work off campus up to 24 hours per week while classes are in session, and full time during scheduled breaks such as summer or winter holidays. On-campus work has no hour limit.
What English score do I need to study in Canada?
Most universities ask for IELTS 6.5 overall (no band below 6.0) for undergraduate programs and 6.5–7.0 for postgraduate. Colleges and diplomas often accept IELTS 6.0. TOEFL, PTE and Duolingo are widely accepted too — check your program's page for exact scores.
When are the intakes for Canadian universities?
The main intake is September (Fall). January (Winter) and May (Summer) are smaller intakes offered by many universities and colleges. Apply 8–12 months before your target intake — popular programs fill early.
What is the Post-Graduation Work Permit (PGWP)?
The PGWP lets international graduates work in Canada after finishing a qualifying program at a designated learning institution. The permit length generally matches your program length, up to 3 years. It is an open work permit — no job offer required to apply.
Can I get permanent residency after studying in Canada?
Many graduates pursue permanent residency through Express Entry (Canadian Experience Class), which rewards Canadian work experience and education. Provincial Nominee Programs (PNPs) also offer study-to-PR streams. There is no guaranteed PR pathway — eligibility depends on points, language scores and policy at the time you apply.
Do I need IELTS if I studied in English before?
Some universities waive the English test if you completed several years of full-time study in English in certain countries — but most applicants still need a test score. Always check the admission requirements of your specific program rather than assuming a waiver applies.

Fees, study permit rules and requirements change — always confirm the latest figures on canada.ca (IRCC) and each university's official site before you apply. Figures above are 2026 approximations compiled from public university and advisory sources.
